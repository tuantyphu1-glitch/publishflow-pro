# EP02 – What Happens When You Stop Rushing to Prove Yourself?
Giọng: tiếng Anh, NAM (Nathan Enhanced). Ngôi thứ nhất. Mốc giờ là bắt đầu mỗi shot (sẽ chỉnh theo độ dài thật của giọng).

[S01] Have you ever tried to get good at something fast, just so nobody would think you were behind?
[S02] I did. For a long time I believed the fastest person wins. When someone landed a trick in front of a crowd, one thought hit me instantly: I have to do that. Today.
[S03] I popped too hard, before my balance was ready. The board went one way. I went the other.
[S04] It was not the concrete that took me down. It was my head, running faster than my feet.
[S05] So I trained twice as hard. Pop, fall, stand up. Pop, fall, stand up. A hundred times. Two hundred.
[S06] But I never fixed my stance. I just repeated the same mistake, faster. The skatepark stopped being a place to learn. It became a race nobody had entered me in.
[S07] And the rush never stays on the board. At home I was building a small bridge for a school project. Skip the tests, glue it fast, finish early, get the praise.
[S08] On demo day it held for two seconds. Then it folded. And I felt the exact same feeling as on the concrete. I was never focused on the work. I was only worried about what people thought.
[S09] So one evening I went back to the park, as the sun went down. Nobody was there to compare myself to. I sat down and asked one simple question: if no one was watching, would I still want to learn this?
[S10] The answer was yes. I just loved the feeling of rolling. The weight I was carrying was never the board. It was the one I put on myself.
[S11] So I dropped the goal of landing the trick right away. I went back to the basics. Stand still. Find my balance. Breathe. Slowly.
[S12] Day by day I saw what I had been missing. My shoulders leaned. My feet pulled back too early because I was afraid to fall. Every wobble stopped being a failure and became a hint.
[S13] Then one evening, when I was not trying to prove anything to anyone, it all lined up. A small bend of the knees. A clean pop. The board spun twice beneath me, and my feet were already there to catch it.
[S14] No one cheered. But something inside me changed for good.
[S15] I went back to the bridge and built it slowly, one tested piece at a time. And it held.
[S16] The biggest lesson was never how to fly. It was how to stay balanced before you jump. The steadiest steps begin the moment you stop rushing.
[S17] But being patient is only the start. There is always a bigger ramp ahead, where fear comes back stronger. Next time, we face it.
