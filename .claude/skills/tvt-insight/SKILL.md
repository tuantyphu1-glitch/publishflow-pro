# TVT Insight — Script Writing Rules

ENGLISH-language YouTube channel on personal finance and behavioral economics.
Completely INDEPENDENT from the Vietnamese channel "TVT Tư Duy" — different language,
different market, different voice. Do NOT apply TVT Tư Duy rules here.

## Core philosophy
"The truth changes behavior. Not motivation."

Every video should make the viewer think one of:
"Why didn't I see this before?" / "I've been thinking about this all wrong." /
"Tomorrow I'm doing this differently." / "I wish I'd known this sooner."

BOUNDARY: never use a blaming tone ("you've been doing it all wrong"). Use evidence,
stories, and credible data to reveal a blind spot so the viewer concludes on their own
that change is needed.

Governing rule: **Don't teach finance. Tell stories that make people care about finance.**
Never open with knowledge — open with a human problem.
Channel formula: REAL PEOPLE + REAL MONEY + REAL PROBLEM + SURPRISING OUTCOME + PRACTICAL LESSON.

## Choosing topics
Never ask "what financial topic should we cover?" Ask: **"What financial problem keeps
people awake at night?"** Every episode must make the viewer ask at least one of:
"How did this happen?" / "Could this happen to me?" / "What would I do?" / "How can I avoid this?"
If it triggers none of the four, the topic isn't worth making.

Generic → specific, always:
- "How To Save Money" → "Why Do You Make More Money But Still Feel Broke?"
- "How To Avoid Scams" → "She Trusted One Phone Call — And Lost Her Life Savings"
- "Retirement Planning" → "He Worked For 40 Years. Then He Realized He Couldn't Afford To Retire."
- "Investing Mistakes" → "He Put His Entire Life Savings Into One Investment."

Seven pain territories to mine: (1) earning more but still broke / lifestyle trap;
(2) fear of losing money — scams, one catastrophic mistake; (3) fear of poverty in old age;
(4) fear of falling back down — millionaires who went broke; (5) wanting wealth but not
knowing where to start; (6) curiosity about how rich people actually behave;
(7) fear of the wrong decision — buy vs rent, pay off mortgage vs invest, "what would you do?"

## Title formula — build a contradiction
Strong finance titles almost always contain a contradiction: **X happened — BUT Y happened.**
"He Earned $1 Million A Year — And Was Still Broke." / "She Saved Money Her Entire Life —
And Still Couldn't Retire." / "The More Money He Made, The More Debt He Had."
The viewer thinks "How is that possible?" — that is the click.

## Open loop
State the outcome up front, withhold the cause. "He Won $314 Million. Five Years Later,
He Had Almost Nothing Left." Do NOT explain why immediately — let the story unfold.
Storytelling always beats "Top 10 financial tips" format.

## Characters must be people, not numbers
Never: "John won $20 million and lost it."
Instead, give the viewer someone to picture, then escalate beat by beat:
"Before winning, John drove a 12-year-old pickup truck. He had never seen more than
$20,000 in his bank account." → "Three months later, he bought a mansion." →
"Then came the calls." → "Friends he hadn't seen in 20 years suddenly wanted to reconnect."

## BEFORE writing episodes 21+
Topics must be researched and PROPOSED to the user for approval first — do not mass-write scripts
without approval. Each proposed topic must map to a REAL behavioral-finance phenomenon or study
(verified via web search, with a named researcher/institution/publication). Propose 10-20 titles
per batch in the existing style ("He/She did X. Y happened." or "Why ___").

Candidate directions not yet used: anchoring bias, overconfidence bias, herd behavior,
gambler's fallacy, scarcity mindset, framing effect, financial literacy gaps, automatic
enrollment/defaults, debt snowball vs avalanche, paradox of choice, confirmation bias, peak-end rule.

## Five mandatory standards for every episode
1. Every lesson traceable to a real source — peer-reviewed research, credible institutions
   (OECD/IMF/World Bank/Federal Reserve/BLS), classic books (The Psychology of Money, Atomic Habits,
   Thinking Fast and Slow, The Millionaire Next Door), real biographies, behavioral research
   (Harvard/Stanford/MIT). If not 100% certain of a quote or figure, use safe phrasing
   ("studies suggest...", "research on X shows..."). NEVER fabricate a source or statistic.
   Always verify via web search before writing.
2. Never lead with theory — open with a shocking fact, number, or situation. Theory comes AFTER the hook.
3. Always target one specific illusion the viewer currently believes, then show contrary evidence.
4. Always include a real story — a character, a mistake or surprising decision, consequences, a lesson.
5. Always close with one concrete action doable WITHIN 24 HOURS.

## Length
3,500-4,500 words per episode — do NOT force it to 5,000. Count and report before submitting:

python3 -c "print(len(open('FILE', encoding='utf-8').read().split()))"

## Ten-episode course arc (applies to EVERY block of 10, including 21-30, 31-40...)
- Episodes 1-4 of each block: break a false belief
- Episodes 5-8: fix a psychological/behavioral error
- Episodes 9-10: build financial foundations (application/synthesis)

## Per-video formula
Five layers: PROBLEM → STORY → REVEAL → LESSON → ACTION.
Expanded: Hook → A shocking truth → The story (real character) → Why the brain gets this
wrong → Research/data → Real-world example → The fix → 24-hour challenge → Tease next episode

## Timeline for an 8-10 minute episode
- 0:00-0:20 HOOK — one unusual, concrete situation. ("He had $8 million in the bank. Nobody knew.")
- 0:20-1:00 THE PROMISE — tell the viewer what they're about to discover.
- 1:00-3:00 STORY — the character, the circumstances, where the money came from.
- 3:00-5:30 THE TURNING POINT — a decision, a mistake, a phone call, an investment, a person.
- 5:30-7:00 THE REVEAL — what actually happened.
- 7:00-8:30 FINANCIAL LESSON — teach here, not earlier. Never "Lesson #1: Save money."
  Aim for the mature framing: "The lesson isn't that money disappears quickly. It's that
  wealth without a system is incredibly fragile."
- 8:30-9:00 TAKEAWAY — one thing the viewer can apply immediately.

## Signature ending
Close with a recognisable line in this register, so viewers know it's TVT Insight without
the logo: "Money can change your life. But understanding money can change what happens after."
/ "The story wasn't really about money. It was about what money revealed."

## Engagement question
Never end on a bare "don't forget to subscribe." Ask a question that doubles as audience
research: "If you suddenly received $10 million tomorrow, what's the first thing you'd do?"
/ "Would you take $1 million today, or $5,000 every month for the rest of your life?"

## Series buckets (use to group episodes into a brand, not a pile of videos)
MONEY THAT DISAPPEARED / MONEY TRAPS (lifestyle, debt, lottery, get-rich-quick, retirement)
/ SCAM STORIES / MONEY PSYCHOLOGY.

## Register
Never try to sound smart. Be useful, be honest, be interesting.
Not: "The macroeconomic implications of excessive consumer leverage..."
Yes: "You earn more money. You spend more money. And somehow, you're still broke."

## Mandatory voice — teleprompter / video-essay style
1. Very short sentences. One idea per line, typically 3-10 words. Break long sentences
   into consecutive short lines.
2. Section headers in ALL CAPS with CONTENT-SPECIFIC names (never "Section 2").
   Fixed order: HOOK / [named story section] / [named brain section — "WHY OUR BRAIN GETS ___ WRONG"]
   / [named research section — "THE RESEARCH BEHIND ___"] / THE BIGGEST LESSON /
   WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU / THE TWENTY-FOUR HOUR CHALLENGE / FINAL MESSAGE / NEXT EPISODE.
3. Consecutive rhetorical questions at the hook and at transitions
   ("The question is not X. The question is Y.").
4. Rule of three — list three items in a row, each on its own line.
5. Simple vocabulary, direct declarative statements.
6. Address the viewer as "you" directly in WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU and THE CHALLENGE.
7. Fixed closing line: "TVT Insight — [short tagline distilling the lesson]".

## Do not reuse (episodes 1-20 already covered)

disposition effect, mental accounting/tax refund, Save More Tomorrow, experiences vs stuff,
compound interest head start, lifestyle inflation, "everyone has debt", dream house,

present bias/future self, The Richest Man in Babylon, savings rate.
